# PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (PRD)

## 1. Product Overview

### Working Product Name
**Teacher Resource Platform**

### Product Vision

To make high-quality teaching and learning resources easily accessible to every teacher, enabling them to spend less time searching for resources and more time preparing effective learning experiences for their students.

### Product Mission

To provide teachers with a trusted, organized and easy-to-use digital library of curriculum-aligned teaching and learning resources for **Primary, JHS, SHS and TVET education**.

### Core Product Promise

> **Find the right resource for the right class, subject and topic—quickly.**

---

# 2. Problem Statement

Teachers often spend significant time searching for appropriate teaching and learning materials.

Resources may be:

- scattered across different sources;
- difficult to locate;
- poorly organized;
- unavailable in convenient formats;
- disconnected from the curriculum;
- difficult to adapt for classroom use;
- mixed with resources intended for different education levels or subjects.

The platform will bring these resources together into one organized environment.

Instead of asking:

> "Where can I find a lesson plan, textbook section, worksheet or assessment for this topic?"

teachers should be able to go directly to:

> **Level → Class → Subject → Topic → Resources**

or simply search for what they need.

---

# 3. Target Users

The primary users are teachers working across four education levels:

### Primary
Teachers teaching lower and upper primary.

### Junior High School
Teachers teaching JHS subjects under the relevant curriculum.

### Senior High School
Teachers teaching SHS subjects and courses.

### TVET
Teachers and instructors delivering vocational and technical education.

### Secondary Users

The platform may also serve:

- school leaders;
- curriculum leaders;
- teacher trainers;
- education organizations;
- education-focused NGOs;
- resource authors;
- publishers;
- approved education partners.

However, the **teacher remains the central user** around whom the product experience is designed.

---

# 4. Product Principles

The platform should be guided by six principles.

### 4.1 Teacher-first

Every major feature should answer:

> "Does this make a teacher's work easier?"

### 4.2 Curriculum-aligned

Resources should be organized around the curriculum rather than simply being stored as a collection of files.

### 4.3 Easy discovery

Teachers should be able to find resources quickly through search or browsing.

### 4.4 Quality and trust

Teachers should know that resources have been reviewed, appropriately classified and clearly identified.

### 4.5 Practical classroom use

The platform should prioritize resources teachers can actually use in preparing and delivering lessons.

### 4.6 Inclusive access

The platform should recognize that teachers have different levels of connectivity, devices, resources and digital experience.

---

# 5. Core Product Experience

A teacher should be able to create an account and indicate:

- education level;
- school/class level;
- subjects taught;
- topics or areas of interest.

The platform then provides a personalized starting point.

For example:

**My Teaching**
- JHS 2
- English Language
- Mathematics
- Science

The teacher can still switch to other levels, classes or subjects whenever needed.

---

# 6. Resource Library

The resource library is the heart of the platform.

It should contain several categories of resources.

### Core Resources

- Digital textbooks
- Teacher guides
- Lesson plans
- Schemes/plans of learning
- Worksheets
- Assessment questions
- Quizzes
- Examination preparation resources
- Classroom activities
- Teaching strategies
- Revision materials
- Student learning materials

### Supporting Resources

- Videos
- Diagrams
- Educational posters
- Presentations
- Reading materials
- Practical activities
- Project ideas
- Remedial resources
- Enrichment resources

The library should continue to expand over time.

---

# 7. Resource Organization

Every resource should have clear information attached to it.

A teacher should be able to see:

- Title
- Education level
- Class/year
- Subject
- Strand/topic
- Resource type
- Curriculum area
- Intended users
- Short description
- Author/publisher
- Date or edition where relevant
- Resource quality/review status

This prevents the library from becoming an unorganized collection of documents.

---

# 8. Resource Discovery

The platform should provide **three complementary discovery methods**.

## 8.1 Search

Teachers can search naturally.

Examples:

> "JHS 2 fractions lesson plan"

> "Primary 5 reading comprehension"

> "SHS Biology photosynthesis"

> "TVET electrical installation worksheet"

Search should understand what the teacher is looking for and surface relevant resources.

---

## 8.2 Browse by Curriculum

Teachers can navigate through the curriculum:

**Education Level → Class → Subject → Strand → Sub-strand → Topic → Resources**

This should be particularly useful for teachers planning lessons systematically.

---

## 8.3 Browse by Subject and Class

Teachers can also choose:

**Level → Class → Subject → Topic**

This provides a simpler route for teachers who do not want to navigate the full curriculum structure.

---

# 9. Personalized Teacher Dashboard

Each teacher should have a personalized home area.

### Suggested sections

**My Subjects**

Subjects the teacher teaches.

**My Classes**

Classes assigned to the teacher.

**Continue Learning**

Resources recently accessed.

**Recently Added**

New resources relevant to the teacher.

**Recommended for You**

Resources based on the teacher's profile and activity.

**Saved Resources**

Resources the teacher has bookmarked.

**My Downloads**

Resources the teacher has downloaded or saved for later use.

---

# 10. Save and Organize Resources

Teachers should be able to save useful resources.

A teacher could create collections such as:

- JHS 1 English
- Term 1 Resources
- BECE Preparation
- Reading Intervention
- Science Practical Activities
- Resources for New Teachers

This allows the platform to become a teacher's **personal resource library**, not just a place they visit occasionally.

---

# 11. Resource Preview

Before accessing a resource, teachers should be able to understand what it contains.

The preview should provide:

- resource description;
- subject;
- class;
- topic;
- resource type;
- number of pages/sections where relevant;
- author/publisher;
- curriculum alignment;
- review status.

Teachers should not have to open several resources before discovering that they are unsuitable.

---

# 12. Download and Offline Access

Because connectivity can vary considerably, the platform should support **offline-friendly access**.

Teachers should be able to save important resources for later use.

The product should particularly prioritize resources that teachers may need repeatedly in classrooms with limited or unreliable internet access.

---

# 13. Recommendations

The platform should recommend resources based on the teacher's selected profile.

For example:

> **You teach JHS 2 English. Here are resources that may help with your upcoming topics.**

Recommendations should consider:

- classes taught;
- subjects taught;
- curriculum areas;
- saved resources;
- recently accessed resources;
- frequently searched topics.

Recommendations should remain helpful rather than overwhelming.

---

# 14. Resource Quality and Review

Trust will be a major differentiator.

Resources should have visible quality indicators such as:

### Reviewed

The resource has been checked against the platform's quality criteria.

### Curriculum-Aligned

The resource has been matched to an identified curriculum area.

### Contributor Resource

The resource has been submitted by an approved contributor.

### Recommended Resource

The resource has been identified as particularly useful based on defined quality criteria.

The platform should avoid allowing an uncontrolled collection of unverified materials to become the main library.

---

# 15. Teacher Feedback

Teachers should be able to provide feedback on resources.

They should be able to indicate:

- useful;
- not useful;
- appropriate for my class;
- needs improvement;
- incorrect/inaccurate;
- outdated;
- inappropriate resource.

They should also be able to report a problem with a resource.

This creates a continuous improvement loop.

---

# 16. Teacher Contributions

The platform should eventually allow teachers and approved education professionals to contribute resources.

A contributor could submit:

- lesson plans;
- worksheets;
- activities;
- assessments;
- teaching strategies;
- original learning materials.

Submitted resources should go through an appropriate review process before becoming part of the main resource library.

This can help the platform grow beyond resources produced by the organization itself.

---

# 17. Publisher and Partner Resources

The platform should eventually provide opportunities for:

- publishers;
- educational organizations;
- NGOs;
- teacher-training organizations;
- curriculum specialists;
- education projects

to contribute or provide approved resources.

Resources should clearly identify their source.

This creates opportunities for partnerships while maintaining trust with teachers.

---

# 18. Accessibility and Inclusion

The platform should be designed for teachers with different needs and circumstances.

Important considerations include:

- simple language and navigation;
- readable resource previews;
- resources suitable for different learner abilities;
- accessibility-conscious materials;
- low-bandwidth-friendly experiences;
- downloadable resources;
- clear categorization.

The goal is to make the platform useful to teachers rather than assuming all teachers have the same digital skills or connectivity.

---

# 19. Assessment Resource Centre

Assessment should be a major resource category.

Teachers should be able to find:

- class exercises;
- quizzes;
- formative assessments;
- end-of-topic assessments;
- revision questions;
- examination-style questions;
- marking guides where appropriate;
- remedial assessments;
- enrichment activities.

Where possible, assessments should be linked to the relevant topic or curriculum area.

---

# 20. Lesson Planning Support

The platform should go beyond simply providing finished lesson plans.

For each topic, teachers could eventually find a **lesson preparation pack** containing:

1. Learning objectives
2. Relevant textbook/resource
3. Suggested lesson plan
4. Teaching activities
5. Learner activities
6. Questions for checking understanding
7. Assessment activity
8. Extension/remedial activity

This would turn the platform from a document repository into a **teaching preparation companion**.

---

# 21. Teaching Strategies

A dedicated teaching-strategy section should provide practical guidance.

For example:

- questioning techniques;
- formative assessment;
- classroom discussion;
- collaborative learning;
- vocabulary development;
- worked examples;
- retrieval practice;
- differentiation;
- classroom management;
- remedial teaching.

Each strategy should explain:

**What it is → Why it matters → How to use it → Example**

---

# 22. Teacher Professional Learning

A future section could provide short professional learning resources.

Examples:

- classroom practice guides;
- short training modules;
- teaching tips;
- instructional videos;
- professional reading;
- CPD resources.

This creates an ecosystem where teachers can find both **what to teach** and **how to teach it effectively**.

---

# 23. Notifications

Notifications should be purposeful rather than excessive.

Teachers could receive notifications about:

- new resources in their subjects;
- newly added curriculum materials;
- saved-resource updates;
- relevant professional learning;
- important platform announcements.

Teachers should have control over what types of notifications they receive.

---

# 24. Community Features

Community features should be introduced carefully.

A future teacher community could allow teachers to:

- share teaching ideas;
- discuss classroom challenges;
- recommend resources;
- share original resources;
- ask professional questions;
- learn from other teachers.

The community should be moderated and focused on professional learning rather than becoming a general social network.

---

# 25. Commercial Model

### Recommended model: Hybrid access

The platform should combine **free access, premium resources and institutional partnerships**.

### Free

A meaningful collection of resources should remain freely accessible.

### Premium

Additional resources could be offered as premium content, such as:

- specialized resource packs;
- advanced assessment collections;
- premium teacher guides;
- curated lesson packages;
- specialized professional learning.

### Institutional

Schools, NGOs, districts and other education organizations could purchase access for groups of teachers.

This model balances **educational access with long-term sustainability**.

---

# 26. Partnerships

The platform should actively pursue partnerships with:

- schools;
- teacher associations;
- publishers;
- NGOs;
- education ministries/agencies;
- teacher-training institutions;
- education projects;
- curriculum specialists.

Partnerships can expand both the **quality and quantity of resources** available.

---

# 27. Content Governance

Because educational resources directly influence classroom teaching, the platform should establish clear content standards.

Every resource should be evaluated for relevant factors such as:

- curriculum relevance;
- educational quality;
- accuracy;
- appropriateness for the intended age group;
- clarity;
- usefulness;
- source/authorship;
- copyright/permission status;
- currency.

The platform should have a clear process for reviewing, updating and removing resources when necessary.

---

# 28. Copyright and Content Rights

The platform should only distribute resources where it has appropriate permission or rights to do so.

Resources should clearly identify:

- copyright holder;
- author;
- publisher;
- permitted use;
- attribution requirements where applicable.

The platform should prioritize **licensed, original, open-licensed or otherwise legitimately distributable materials**.

---

# 29. Key User Journeys

## Journey 1: Finding a lesson resource

Teacher logs in.

→ Selects JHS 2 English  
→ Searches "comprehension"  
→ Reviews available resources  
→ Opens a resource  
→ Saves it  
→ Uses/downloads it for lesson preparation.

---

## Journey 2: Curriculum browsing

Teacher selects:

**JHS → English → Strand → Sub-strand → Topic**

The platform displays all relevant resources.

Teacher chooses:

**Lesson Plan + Worksheet + Assessment**

and saves them together.

---

## Journey 3: Preparing a lesson

Teacher searches for a topic.

The platform presents a **resource pack** containing:

- teacher guide;
- textbook material;
- lesson plan;
- classroom activity;
- worksheet;
- assessment.

The teacher can save the complete pack for future use.

---

## Journey 4: Discovering new resources

Teacher logs in.

The dashboard displays:

**New resources for your subjects**

The teacher sees newly added resources relevant to their classes and saves those they find useful.

# 33. Success Metrics

The platform should measure whether it is genuinely helping teachers.

### Access

- Number of registered teachers
- Active teachers
- Schools represented
- Education levels represented

### Resource discovery

- Searches performed
- Resources viewed
- Search-to-resource success rate
- Most requested subjects/topics

### Resource use

- Resources saved
- Resources downloaded/accessed
- Frequently used resources
- Repeat usage

### Teacher satisfaction

- Resource usefulness ratings
- Teacher feedback
- Reported resource problems
- Teacher retention

### Impact indicators

Over time, the platform should investigate whether teachers report:

- reduced preparation time;
- easier access to teaching materials;
- improved lesson preparation;
- greater use of varied teaching resources;
- increased confidence in teaching particular topics.

# 34. Product Differentiation

The platform should not compete simply on **having the largest number of files**.

Its differentiation should be:

> **Relevant resources, organized around the curriculum, designed for teachers, and easy to find.**

A teacher should not need to search through hundreds of unrelated documents.

The platform should understand:

**Who I am → What I teach → Who I teach → What I am teaching now → What resources can help me?**

# 36. Product Positioning

The platform should be positioned as:

> **The teacher's digital resource library and lesson preparation companion.**

Not merely:

> "An online textbook platform."

The distinction is important.

A textbook platform answers:

> **"Where can I read this book?"**

Your platform should answer:

> **"I am teaching this class and this topic. What do I need to teach it well?"**

That broader proposition gives the product significantly more room to grow.

---

# 37. Recommended Long-Term Vision

The long-term product could become an **education resource ecosystem** connecting:

**Teachers ↔ Resources ↔ Curriculum ↔ Publishers ↔ Education Organizations ↔ Professional Learning**

At maturity, a teacher could come to the platform at the beginning of the term and use it throughout the teaching cycle:

**Plan → Prepare → Teach → Assess → Reflect → Improve**

That should be the ultimate product vision.

# 39. Core Product Statement

**For teachers across Primary, JHS, SHS and TVET who spend too much time searching for appropriate teaching resources, the Teacher Resource Platform is a curriculum-aligned digital resource library and lesson-preparation companion that brings textbooks, teacher guides, lesson plans, worksheets, assessments and other teaching resources into one easy-to-navigate platform.**

Unlike a conventional digital library, it organizes resources around the **teacher's actual classroom needs**, enabling teachers to search, browse, save and access relevant resources for their classes and topics.
